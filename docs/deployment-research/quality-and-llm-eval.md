Scope: **output quality**, not infra performance (latency/uptime/cost — see the separate ops report). Audience: the next engineer picking this up. Grounded in the actual code as of 2026-08-13; external claims are cited; anything not verified against a primary source is labeled **[UNCONFIRMED]**.

## 0. Where this picks up

A prior session hardened two fail-open paths into fail-closed ones:

## 1. LLM-as-judge evaluation for this pipeline

### 1.1 What Tayari's own scorers already compute (read directly from source)

Before adding an LLM judge, know what's already measured, because it changes what the judge needs to add:

**`ats_engine.heuristic_ats_score`** (`backend/python/app/services/ats_engine.py:243`) — fully deterministic, no LLM: weighted checklist (contact info, sections present, word count band, bullet count, action-verb hits, quantified-achievement regex count, dates, recency, keyword/phrase overlap with the JD via `_tokenize`/`_bigrams`, job-title alignment). Produces a 0–100 score plus a `per_ats` reweighted estimate for Workday/Greenhouse/iCIMS profiles with a confidence band. This is a **structural/formatting linter**, not a judgment of writing quality or truthfulness.

**`ats_engine.semantic_similarity_score`** — pure-Python TF-IDF cosine similarity (`_tfidf_cosine_similarity`) between resume and JD text, 0–100. No embeddings, no synonym awareness (the `tayari-quality-signal-campaign` skill's own S2 solution item flags this: "built ML models" vs "constructed machine-learning systems" score as dissimilar).

**`optimizer.validate_master_alignment`** — a structural fabrication check (not an LLM judge): parses tailored vs. master resume, flags any skill or certification in the tailored version absent from the master. Fails closed on parser exception (§0). It catches *invented credentials*, not *invented accomplishments within an existing bullet* (e.g., embellishing "helped launch" into "led the launch of a $2M initiative" — same skill vocabulary, fabricated scope) — that class is currently uncaught by anything in the pipeline.

**`guardrails/truthfulness.check_truthfulness`** — regex-based factual-drift detector: employers dropped, years/dates/degrees invented, email changed, severe truncation. Same limitation as above: it anchors on *named entities and numbers*, not on claims about scope, impact, or causation.

**`optimizer.optimize_with_reflection`**'s reflexion loop uses `heuristic_ats_score` as its **only** optimization target (`SCORE_TARGET = 85`). This is the crux of the existing quality-signal problem the campaign skill documents: the loop is provably self-consistent (it optimizes toward a number it also computes) but has no external validity check today.

None of the above is an LLM judge. **There is currently zero LLM-based quality scoring anywhere in the resume/cover-letter pipeline** — every "score" a user sees is either a deterministic heuristic or a raw LLM generation with no evaluation layer behind it.

### 1.2 Current best practice for LLM-as-judge at production scale (2026)

**Sampling rate.** Sample 5–20% of production traffic uniformly, plus **100% of**: errors, outputs below a heuristic-score threshold, and cost/latency outliers. Reduce below 20% (or swap to a cheaper distilled judge) once judge cost exceeds ~25% of total inference spend — the marginal coverage stops paying for itself past that point (FutureAGI, "LLM-as-Judge Best Practices in 2026"). Even a calibrated judge drifts, so cross-check 5–10% of its own scores against a frontier judge or a human reviewer on a rolling basis.

**Judge model choice.** Use a **different model family than the generator** to avoid self-preference/shared-blind-spot bias (see below) — Tayari's generator is whatever `LLM_PROVIDER`/`LLM_MODEL` resolves to (Ollama, OpenRouter, etc. per `llm_service.py`), so the judge should be pinned to a distinct family, not "the same provider's next-tier model." A tiered strategy is standard: a frontier-class judge (GPT-5/Opus/Gemini-3-Pro class) as a calibration anchor for CI gates and periodic re-calibration, and a cheaper flash/mini-class judge (calibrated against the frontier judge on your own data) for the bulk of production span-scoring (FutureAGI 2026; DeepEval "LLM-as-a-Judge in 2026").

**Rubric design.** Criteria should be MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive) and atomic — one criterion, one judgment, no stacked concepts — because vague or overlapping criteria produce inconsistent scores and compressed distributions that can't separate good from great (Appen, "LLM-as-a-Judge Rubric Design"; PReMISE, arXiv:2605.30803).

**The shared-blind-spot trap (the one this task specifically flagged).** This is empirically real, not a hypothetical: when judge and generator share a model family, they share training-data distributions and inductive biases, so the judge can systematically fail to catch the exact class of error the generator is prone to making — inflating perceived quality (arXiv:2606.10315, "Catching One in Five: LLM-as-Judge Blind Spots"; emergentmind summary of arXiv:2504.03846 on self-preference bias). The mitigating research finding: self-preference is partly *legitimate* (better generators tend to be better judges of that same task) but *harmful* self-preference persists specifically on task instances where the evaluator itself would perform poorly as a generator — i.e., the failure concentrates exactly where you'd worst want it to (arXiv:2604.22891). Practical implication for Tayari: **a judge from the same family/provider as the optimizer's generator is not just weaker, it is weakest precisely on fabricated-but-fluent resume content — the failure mode this project has already found in production twice** (`lessons.md` 2026-08-13 entry; the mock-masking pattern in `llm_service.py`). Tool-augmented judges (i.e., a judge that can check a claim against the master resume text, not just read the generated text) are less susceptible to this because they're not relying on the same learned-heuristic pattern matching the generator used to fabricate.

### 1.3 Proposed rubric structure

**Resume-optimization quality judge** — invoked on the *final* `optimized_text` from `optimize_with_reflection`, given `{original_resume, job_description, optimized_text, alignment_report, guardrails}` as context (not asked to re-derive what the deterministic checks already compute):

| Dimension | Question the judge answers | Why it's not already covered |
|---|---|---|
| Grounded specificity | Does every claim of scope/impact/ownership trace to something stated or reasonably inferable from the original resume — not just named entities (which `truthfulness.py` already checks) but *degree of embellishment* on existing bullets? | `validate_master_alignment` and `check_truthfulness` are entity/date-anchored; they miss scope inflation on real bullets |
| Recruiter-perceived quality | Would a human recruiter, reading this cold, judge it as competent professional writing (not AI-buzzword generic, not keyword-stuffed, coherent narrative)? | `heuristic_ats_score` is structural; `remove_ai_buzzwords` is a fixed blocklist, not a fluency judgment |
| JD relevance (semantic, not lexical) | Does the resume's *substance* — not just token overlap — address what this JD is actually asking for? | `semantic_similarity_score` is TF-IDF; misses synonymy and misses "keywords present but irrelevant context" |
| STAR/impact completeness | Are the top 3–5 bullets result-oriented with real (or explicitly `[ESTIMATE]`-tagged) outcomes, not just verb-quantifier padding? | `_score_bullet_star` is a regex heuristic (starts-with-verb, has-a-number) — doesn't judge whether the "result" is meaningful |
| Truthfulness (adjudicated) | Given the original resume as ground truth, does the optimized version contain any claim a reasonable reader would find misleading? | The judge here should be **tool-augmented**: given both texts, not just the output — this directly counters the shared-blind-spot risk in §1.2 by grounding the judgment in a diff, not free recall |

Score each 1–5 with a one-sentence justification (justification-then-score ordering, not score-then-justify, per rubric literature). Emit a **fail flag**, not just a low score, when Truthfulness or Grounded Specificity drops below 3 — this is the dimension that should page someone, not just move a dashboard average.

**Cover-letter quality judge** — no cover-letter equivalent of `ats_engine.py` exists yet in what was read; propose an analogous rubric: (1) Grounded specificity (same fabrication risk, arguably higher for cover letters since they're more narrative/less bullet-constrained), (2) JD relevance, (3) Voice/tone appropriateness for the target company (a dimension resumes don't need), (4) Structural completeness (opening hook, 2–3 body paragraphs with evidence, closing call-to-action), (5) Non-genericness — flag the same boilerplate phrase set used across a user's multiple letters (a check with no code equivalent today; would need cross-letter comparison, not per-letter).

**ATS-fit score, interview-prep questions, filled forms** — lower priority for an LLM judge specifically:
- ATS-fit score is already deterministic/heuristic (`heuristic_ats_score`, `evaluate_5d_fit`) with no LLM step — the trust question there is "does the heuristic correlate with real ATS behavior," which is a §1.4/§3 ground-truth problem, not a judge-the-generation problem.
- Interview-prep questions: an LLM judge should check for role/seniority relevance and non-repetition across a session — lower stakes than resume/cover-letter since there's no fabrication-about-the-user risk.
- Filled application forms: this is an *action-correctness* problem (did the agent put the right value in the right field), which is closer to the browser-automation agent's own success/failure telemetry than to text-quality judging — out of scope for a text-quality judge, but should have its own field-level accuracy check (compare submitted values against source-of-truth profile data) that is a diff, not a judgment call, so it doesn't need an LLM at all.

### 1.4 How to avoid the judge being gamed

1. **Different model family from the generator, hard requirement, not a preference** — enforce this in config, not just in the initial rollout (a future engineer changing `LLM_PROVIDER` for the generator must not silently also move the judge to the same family).
2. **Ground the judge in a diff, not free recall** — for truthfulness/grounded-specificity specifically, pass both `original_text` and `optimized_text` and ask for a structured diff-style judgment, not "rate this resume." This is the direct fix for the shared-blind-spot failure mode research identifies.
3. **Periodic re-anchoring against a frontier judge and/or human labels** — per §1.2, even a well-chosen judge drifts; 5–10% of judge scores should be cross-checked against a stronger/different judge or a human reviewer on a rolling cadence (weekly is a reasonable starting cadence given production volume is presumably still low).
4. **Golden-set regression gate in CI** — the repo already has `eval/datasets/ats_scoring_v1.yaml` and `eval/datasets/tayari_resume_v1.yaml` (`backend/python/eval/`) plus `eval/runner.py`. Add fixed input/expected-judge-verdict pairs (including at least a few deliberately-fabricated resumes) to this suite and gate merges on the judge not regressing past a threshold (±3% on key metrics is the cited industry norm — arXiv:2606.10315 lineage, FutureAGI's LLM-regression-testing guide) — **and pin the judge model version explicitly**, the same way §3.4 recommends pinning it for drift detection, so a provider-side model update doesn't silently change what "pass" means.
5. **Never let the judge replace the deterministic fabrication checks** — `validate_master_alignment` and `check_truthfulness` should stay as hard gates regardless of judge score; the judge is an *additional* quality signal, not a replacement authority, because a regex-anchored entity check is strictly harder to fool with fluent prose than an LLM judge is.

Sources: [LLM-as-Judge Best Practices in 2026 (FutureAGI)](https://futureagi.com/blog/llm-as-judge-best-practices-2026) · [LLM-as-a-Judge in 2026: Top evaluation techniques (DeepEval)](https://deepeval.com/blog/llm-as-a-judge) · [Catching One in Five: LLM-as-Judge Blind Spots in Production Multi-Turn (arXiv:2606.10315)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10315) · [Quantifying and Mitigating Self-Preference Bias of LLM Judges (arXiv:2604.22891)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22891v2) · [LLM Evaluators: Self-Preference Bias summary of arXiv:2504.03846](https://api.emergentmind.com/papers/2504.03846) · [LLM-as-a-Judge Rubric Design (Appen)](https://www.appen.com/llm-as-a-judge-rubric-design) · [PReMISE: Policy Rubrics as Measurement Specifications (arXiv:2605.30803)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30803) · [LLM Regression Testing Guide 2026 (FutureAGI)](https://futureagi.com/glossary/llm-regression-testing/)

## 2. A/B and outcome-based evaluation

### 2.1 What exists today (read directly from source)

**Schema** (`backend/db/migrations/20260626_predictive_analytics.sql`): `resume_variants` (id, resume_id, name, original_text) and `ab_testing_bandit` (variant_id, pulls, conversions — one row per variant, unique on variant_id). `applications.resume_variant_id` links an application to the variant sent.

**Real outcome tracking** (`backend/db/migrations/20260731_social_privacy_preferences.sql:100`): `application_outcomes` — one row per application (`UNIQUE(application_id)`), booleans for `recruiter_reply`, `phone_screen`, `technical_interview`, `final_interview`, `offer_received`, `offer_accepted`, plus `salary_offered`, `outcome_date`. RLS-scoped per user (`20260731_social_rls_hardening.sql`). This table is the correct real-outcome ledger — it exists, is reasonably complete for a funnel, and is already wired into `analytics_service.py`'s conversion funnel (per the `lessons.md` 2026-08-13 fix, which made the funnel return real zeros instead of a synthetic 11-application funnel when no data exists).

**The bandit loop is not connected to any of this.** `bandit_service.py`'s `_ARM_STATS` is a **process-memory dict**, explicitly documented in its own module docstring as lost on restart, with a comment that persistent stats living in `application_outcomes` and being "re-hydrated on startup" is a **future** M8 iteration — not implemented. Confirmed by grep: `select_strategy` and `record_outcome` (the functions with real epsilon-greedy logic and the honest "learning" vs. "optimized" cold-start gate at `min_samples_threshold=20`) have **zero callers outside `app/tests/`**. The only production-wired path is `app/api/predictive.py`'s `POST /api/v1/predictive/bandit/select`, which calls `BanditService.select_variant` — a **stateless, single-call argmax** over whatever `pulls`/`conversions` the caller happens to pass in that request; it reads nothing from and writes nothing to `resume_variants` or `ab_testing_bandit`. Its own docstring claims "Applies Thompson Sampling" — it does not; there's no posterior distribution or probabilistic draw anywhere in `select_variant`'s ~10 lines, it's a plain max().

**Net finding**: the schema for closing the loop exists (variant table, bandit stats table, outcome table, and the FK linking application → variant), but nothing in the running system writes to `ab_testing_bandit` or reads `application_outcomes` back into a variant-selection decision. The bandit "loop" is currently open at both ends — no variant assignment is persisted per application in a queryable way beyond the FK, and no outcome feeds back into arm selection.

### 2.2 Closing the loop — schema and pipeline

**Schema-level (mostly present, needs wiring not new tables):**
1. On resume optimization, when a variant is generated for a specific `role_family`/`strategy` (matching `bandit_service`'s existing key shape), insert a `resume_variants` row and — this is the missing link — **also write the `(user_id, role_family, strategy)` triple into a column on `resume_variants` or a join row**, so a later outcome can be traced back to which arm produced it without re-deriving it from free text. `resume_variants` today has no `role_family`/`strategy` columns; either add them or add a small `resume_variant_strategy` mapping table.
2. When `application_outcomes` gets a row (recruiter_reply / interview / offer), that's the reward signal. It already FKs to `applications.application_id`, and `applications.resume_variant_id` already FKs to `resume_variants` — so **the join to go from outcome → variant → strategy already exists structurally**; what's missing is the code path that (a) actually calls `record_outcome` when an `application_outcomes` row changes, and (b) makes `select_strategy` read its `_ARM_STATS` from a DB query over that join instead of the in-memory dict, so state survives restarts and is shared across processes/workers (Celery workers and the FastAPI process are separate processes today — an in-memory dict can't be consistent between them regardless of restarts).

**Pipeline-level:**
1. Replace `_ARM_STATS` with a DB-backed read in `_get_arm_stats` (query `ab_testing_bandit` joined through `resume_variants`/`applications`/`application_outcomes` filtered by `user_id`), keeping the same per-user isolation invariant the module's SECURITY NOTE already calls load-bearing.
2. Add a webhook/callback or scheduled reconciliation job that calls `bandit_service.record_outcome(...)` whenever `application_outcomes` is written or updated (e.g., a DB trigger emitting an event, or the endpoint that lets a user mark "got an interview" also calling `record_outcome` synchronously) — reward signal has to flow into the bandit, not just sit in the outcomes table.
3. Fix `predictive.py`'s docstring or fix the implementation: either actually implement Thompson sampling (Beta-distribution posterior draw per arm from `pulls`/`conversions`) in `select_variant`, or stop calling it that — the current mismatch between the docstring's claim and the code is exactly the kind of "looks more real than it is" gap this whole campaign exists to close.
4. Keep `select_strategy`'s existing cold-start honesty gate (`status: "learning"` below `min_samples_threshold=20`, `is_honest: True` field) — this is a good pattern already in the code; make sure whatever UI eventually surfaces bandit results (none does today, confirmed by grep) respects that flag and doesn't render "Optimized" claims before n=20.

### 2.3 Sample size before replacing `predictive_scorer.py`'s heuristic with a trained model

`predictive_scorer.py` states its own limitation directly in its docstring: "NOT a trained model: no application outcomes are used to fit these weights, so the output is a structural quality signal, not a callback probability." That's accurate and should stay true until there's real labeled data to fit against.

**Research on required sample size for this class of problem** (binary-outcome supervised classification from tabular/text-derived features):

- General ML rule of thumb: samples ≫ features, commonly cited ratios of 50–100x features as a floor, up to 1000x for robust generalization ([Engora Data Blog, "Don't stop till you get enough"](https://blog.engora.com/2025/06/dont-stop-till-you-get-enough-sample.html)).
- Closest matching empirical reference class found — clinical binary-outcome prediction models built with ML: a systematic review found a **median training sample size of 310** (range 54–57,134) with a **median of only 87 positive events** (range 25–2,544) across published models ([Journal of Clinical Epidemiology systematic review](https://www.jclinepi.com/article/S0895-4356(25)00008-3/fulltext)). The same review's headline finding is that this median is likely **too small** — models in the review under-performed with more data added, i.e. published models were frequently underpowered even at these sizes.
- Algorithm sensitivity: boosting needed 2–3x the "recommended" sample size to hit target performance in one study; random forests/bagging failed to hit target performance even at 12x the naive recommendation ([PMC12308042, tree-ensemble sample size study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12308042/)).

**Applied to Tayari**: "interview obtained" / "offer obtained" are rarer positive-class events than most clinical binary outcomes (real-world interview rate per application is commonly in the single-digit-to-low-teens percent range — Tayari's own data will set the true base rate once `application_outcomes` has volume). Using the clinical median (~310 total / ~87 events) as a **floor, not a target**, and given the algorithm-sensitivity finding above, a defensible internal target is **at least 300–500 positive outcome events (interviews or better) per model, spread across enough distinct resume/JD/strategy combinations that the model isn't just memorizing one user's pattern** — with the explicit caveat that this number is extrapolated from a different domain (clinical prediction) because no resume-callback-specific sample-size literature surfaced in search; label this estimate accordingly when citing it internally. Below that, keep `predictive_scorer.py`'s heuristic and be explicit in the UI that it's structural, not learned — which the code already is, so the discipline here is "don't remove that honesty as a growth-hacking move before the data justifies it."

**Precondition that must exist before any of this is viable**: `application_outcomes` needs actual volume and, critically, needs **non-outcome rows** (i.e., users must report *rejections/no-replies* too, not just successes) or the training set will be success-survivorship-biased — check whether the current UI/flow that populates `application_outcomes` prompts for negative outcomes at all; this wasn't verified in this pass and should be checked before data collection is assumed to be unbiased.

Sources: [Larger sample sizes are needed for ML clinical prediction models (J. Clin. Epi. systematic review)](https://www.jclinepi.com/article/S0895-4356(25)00008-3/fulltext) · [Evaluating sample size requirements of tree-ensemble ML for clinical risk prediction (PMC12308042)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12308042/) · [Sample size in machine learning (Engora)](https://blog.engora.com/2025/06/dont-stop-till-you-get-enough-sample.html) · [A/B Test Sample Size Calculator methodology (Statsig)](https://www.statsig.com/calculator) · [Minimum Detectable Effect explainer (SplitMetrics)](https://splitmetrics.com/resources/minimum-detectable-effect-mde/)

For the **A/B test between two resume variants specifically** (not the trained-model question, a separate simpler statistical test): standard power analysis applies — inputs are baseline conversion rate (interview rate), minimum detectable effect (MDE), significance level (conventionally 0.05), and power (conventionally 0.80); smaller MDE or lower baseline rate both drive the required sample size up sharply, and sample size is the dominant lever for power. Given Tayari's likely low per-user application volume, **per-user A/B testing of resume variants is probably underpowered by individual users** — the bandit-across-users design already in `bandit_service.py` (keyed by `role_family`/`strategy`, pooling across users within a role family, not per-user) is the right shape for this reason; per-user variant selection alone won't reach significance.

## 3. Guardrail and drift monitoring

### 3.1 What to log per guardrail run (from `PipelineGate.check`, `gate.py`)

Every call already returns a structured `{all_passed, results: {truthfulness, keyword_stuffing, pii}}`. Log, per call, at minimum: `trace_id`, timestamp, which of the three checks ran vs. was skipped (`verified` field for truthfulness — already added), pass/fail per check, and for truthfulness specifically whether it ran in `verified: True` or `verified: False` mode (the fail-closed path added in §0 — if this ever spikes, it means `original_text` stopped being passed somewhere, which is itself a regression worth alerting on independent of pass/fail rates).

### 3.2 Guardrail trip-rate monitoring

Track, time-bucketed (daily is a reasonable starting granularity given expected volume):
- **Block rate per guardrail** (truthfulness / keyword_stuffing / pii), not just an aggregate `all_passed` rate — the three checks have different root causes when they trip, so an aggregate hides which one moved.
- **Trend, not just level.** A rising truthfulness-block rate over a week is the signal called out in the task: it means either (a) users are pushing the optimizer harder into fabrication territory (adversarial prompting, master resumes with deliberately inflated content) or (b) a regression in the generator (prompt change, provider swap, temperature change) increased fabrication rate. Distinguishing (a) from (b) requires correlating the trip-rate trend against deploy timestamps and provider/model-version metadata (§3.4) — log both alongside the trip event.
- **Keyword-stuffing trip rate** is a reasonable proxy for "users gaming the optimizer" specifically, since it's the check most directly defeated by adversarial input (stuff the master resume, let the optimizer echo it) rather than by a generator regression.

Industry framing for what "acceptable" looks like: false-positive rate under 5% is commonly cited as a production floor, with 2026 target benchmarks tightening toward false-positive rate < 2%, MTTD < 5 min, MTTR < 15 min — though a cited cross-implementation study found real guardrail false-positive rates ranging from 0.1% to 13.1%, so these are aspirational anchors, not universal norms ([QASkills.sh, "Testing an LLM Guardrail's False-Positive Rate"](https://qaskills.sh/blog/testing-guardrail-false-positive-rate); [AI Safety Directory, "LLM Guardrails: The Complete Guide (2026)"](https://aisecurityandsafety.org/en/guides/llm-guardrails/)).

### 3.3 False-positive rate (guardrail blocks something legitimate)

None of the three guardrails currently emit a confidence score alongside pass/fail (`truthfulness.py`, `keyword_stuffing.py`, `pii_detector.py` are all boolean-gate regex checks) — so false-positive measurement here has to come from **sampled human review of blocks**, not from the guardrail's own output. Concretely: sample a fixed percentage (start at 100% while volume is low, dial down as volume grows) of `passed: False` events per guardrail into a review queue; a human (or, per §1, a tool-augmented LLM judge different from the generator) labels each as true-positive or false-positive. Track this rate the same way as trip rate — trending, time-bucketed, correlated with deploys.

Specific known false-positive risk in the current code: `truthfulness.py`'s "employers should not disappear without reason" check fires on `len(missing_employers) >= 3` — a legitimate resume trim (e.g., dropping short-tenure roles from >10 years ago per standard resume-writing advice) could trip this. This is a plausible, not confirmed, false-positive source — flag it for the review queue specifically rather than assuming it's fine.

### 3.4 Detecting silent LLM provider degradation

This is the failure class `lessons.md`'s mock-masking findings are adjacent to, but distinct from: mock-masking is "no provider configured, code fabricates"; this section is "a provider *is* configured and responding, but it quietly got worse or got swapped underneath you" — e.g., an API-compatible endpoint silently downgrades the served model, or a provider ships a model update that changes behavior without a version bump you'd notice.

Concrete mechanisms, in priority order for this codebase:

1. **Pin and log model identity per call.** `llm_service.py`'s `active_engine_label()` per provider should be logged on every `llm_complete` call (trace_id, timestamp, engine label, tier). If a provider's API silently swaps models while reporting the same label, this alone won't catch it — that's what #2 is for — but it makes "did we change providers/tiers" trivially answerable when a quality-metric alarm fires, instead of requiring an engineer to manually reconstruct config history.
2. **Golden-set canary probes** — a small, fixed set of prompts (order of 10–30) with known-stable expected characteristics, run on a schedule (e.g., hourly or daily) against the same provider/model config, scored by the same heuristic checks (`heuristic_ats_score`, `check_truthfulness`, `check_keyword_stuffing`) already in the codebase — no new scoring logic needed, just a scheduled job that feeds fixed inputs through the existing deterministic scorers and alerts if the score distribution shifts outside a threshold band. This is the direct mechanism recommended for catching silent provider-side regressions: lock the exact model version for both generator and judge so a provider-side update is visible in the eval rather than silently absorbed ([FutureAGI, LLM Regression Testing 2026](https://futureagi.com/glossary/llm-regression-testing/); [DigitalOcean, "The Silent Versioning Problem in AI Inference"](https://www.digitalocean.com/community/tutorials/model-silent-versioning-problem)).
3. **Behavioral fingerprinting for cases where provider identity itself is in question** (e.g., a self-hosted/proxied endpoint where you can't fully trust the provider's self-reported model string) — a small forensic prompt set whose response characteristics are stable per real model, used to verify the endpoint is serving what it claims. Lower priority for Tayari today since providers are configured directly (Ollama, OpenRouter — see `backend/python/CLAUDE.md`), not resold/proxied, but worth having if a cheaper reseller-style provider is ever added. Reference implementation exists as an OSS pattern (32 forensic prompts): [llm-verify (GitHub)](https://github.com/mintesnot-teshome/llm-verify).
4. **Track the LLM-as-judge score trend itself** (once §1 is built) as a second, independent signal — a real quality drop should show up in both the deterministic canary probes (#2) and the judge's score trend; if only one moves, that's diagnostic information about whether the regression is structural (heuristic-visible) or semantic/fluency-only (judge-visible).

## 4. Minimal weekly dashboard (8–12 numbers, non-technical product owner)

Design constraint from `lessons.md`'s own retrospective: every number below must have an explicit "no data" / "N/A" state that is *visually distinct* from a real zero or a real pass — the root cause of all five 2026-08-13 fabrication bugs was a degraded path rendering identically to a successful one. Each metric's "N/A" state should be a missing key or a distinct status string, per that entry's own reusable lesson, not a defaulted number.

| # | Metric | Why it belongs on THIS dashboard (reality vs. theatre) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real applications with a tracked outcome this week** (count, from `application_outcomes`, must include negatives/no-replies, not just successes) | The denominator that makes every rate below honest. If this is 0 or near-0, every rate below is theatre — it should read "insufficient data," not silently compute a rate over synthetic or empty data (this is the exact bug the analytics-service fix closed 2026-08-13). |
| 2 | **Real interview rate** (`phone_screen` OR `technical_interview` OR `final_interview` = true / #1) | This is the literal metric behind the promise "we got you more callbacks." Must be computed only from real `application_outcomes` rows — never from a fallback/sample funnel. |
| 3 | **Real offer rate** (`offer_received` / #1) | Same as above, one funnel stage deeper — this is the actual bottom-line proof point. |
| 4 | **Optimized vs. un-optimized interview-rate delta** (requires resume_variant_id join per §2.2 — until that's wired, this row should read "not yet measurable" explicitly, not be omitted or estimated) | This is the single number that answers "does optimization actually work," as opposed to "does the optimizer produce plausible-looking text." Until the bandit loop closes (§2.2), this metric literally cannot be computed honestly — the dashboard should say so, loudly, rather than hide the gap. |
| 5 | **LLM-as-judge mean truthfulness/grounded-specificity score, trailing 7d** (once §1 ships) | Direct proxy for "is the optimizer fabricating," independent of the regex-based guardrails — a second, differently-blind-spotted signal. |
| 6 | **Guardrail trip rate by check (truthfulness / keyword-stuffing / pii), trailing 7d, with week-over-week delta** | A rising trend is the early warning for either gaming or regression (§3.2) — the delta matters more than the level for a weekly review cadence. |
| 7 | **% of optimizer runs where truthfulness ran unverified** (`verified: False` from `gate.py`, i.e., `original_text` missing) | This should be ~0%. Any nonzero, sustained rate means a caller stopped passing the original resume — a silent regression in the guardrail's own coverage, not a resume-quality issue, and easy to miss if it's not its own line. |
| 8 | **Guardrail false-positive rate, sampled** (§3.3) | Without this, "guardrail trip rate went up" is ambiguous between "we're catching more real problems" and "we're wrongly blocking legitimate resumes" — this line disambiguates it. |
| 9 | **Mock/fallback rate — % of optimizer calls that hit `MockProvider` or the LLM-call exception fallback (`optimized = resume_text` in `optimizer.py`'s `except Exception` branch)** | Direct instrumentation of the mock-masking failure mode this whole campaign is about. Should be logged already via `active_engine_label()` (§3.4) — surfacing it here makes it impossible for "the LLM engine silently wasn't configured/failed" to hide behind a clean-looking week of other numbers. |
| 10 | **Canary/golden-set score, trailing reading vs. 30-day baseline band** (§3.4) | The product owner doesn't need the raw canary score, just "in band" / "out of band" — this is the tripwire for silent provider degradation reaching users before a customer complaint does. |
| 11 | **Bandit arm status: "learning" vs. "optimized" count per role_family** (once §2.2 wires `select_strategy` into production) | Directly surfaces the existing cold-start honesty gate (`min_samples_threshold=20`, `is_honest: True` already in `bandit_service.py`) at the product level — prevents a future dashboard or UI from claiming "optimized" before n=20, which is exactly the kind of premature-confidence claim this campaign exists to prevent. |
| 12 | **Sample-data / fallback badge trigger count** (from `ApplicationAnalytics.tsx`'s amber "Sample Data" badge and the "Illustrative — not your data" labels fixed 2026-08-13) | A meta-metric: how often are users actually seeing real numbers vs. an honestly-labeled placeholder. Should trend toward 0 as real usage grows; a sustained nonzero rate here means the product isn't yet generating enough real data for its own headline promise to be provable — which the product owner needs to know plainly, not have obscured by a dashboard that only shows the "clean" numbers. |

Rows 1–4 are the outcome-truth core; rows 5–8 are the quality/guardrail layer; rows 9–10 are the "is the LLM even real and stable" layer; rows 11–12 are meta-honesty checks that specifically exist because of this codebase's fabrication history, not generic best practice — they're the direct answer to the task's framing that "the dashboard's job is to make it impossible for a future fabrication bug to hide again."
