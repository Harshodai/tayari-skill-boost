Answering your actual question

"Will this platform be profitable?" — Profitability is nearly guaranteed if you get users, because your costs are almost zero: no team, no paid ads yet, and an LLM bill users can carry themselves (BYO-key or Ollama). Ten paying customers makes you profitable in the accounting sense. The real question isn't profit, it's scale: does this become $3K/mo, $30K/mo, or nothing? That's decided entirely by distribution, and today your distribution score is 5/100.

"Many tools do this — is there room?" Yes, and the crowding is misleading. Rezi ($3.2M, bootstrapped) and Teal (~$5M on $20.7M raised) coexist while Jobscan charges $49.95 and Jobright just raised prices to $39.99. Prices rising in a crowded market means demand exceeds supply of good products. Sonara died, but from bad product-market fit, not saturation. The category kills the undifferentiated and the undistributed — you're differentiated and undistributed, so fix the second thing.

The one number that should scare you: at 15% monthly churn — normal for job-search tools because success removes the customer — holding 500 subscribers means acquiring 75 new paying customers every single month, forever. Not 500 once. 75 per month, permanently. That treadmill, not your code, is the business. Everything in your repo is now a rounding error next to whether you can build a repeatable acquisition engine.
You didn't build a product that can't make money. You built a product that can't be found — and then spent a month making it better instead of findable.

The churn trap — the structural flaw in this entire category

This is the thing nobody selling you a "job tool business plan" says out loud: your product works, so your customer leaves. Job search is an acute need, not a chronic one. Success is churn. Failure is also churn. Every subscriber is a departing subscriber.

Churn/moAvg customer lifeGross LTV @ $19Reality
20%5.0 months$95Typical job-tool behavior — search ends, subscription ends.
14%7.1 months$136Realistic with a strong tracker keeping people engaged post-hire.
10%10 months$190Requires genuine post-hire value (see mitigations).

Context: healthy prosumer SaaS runs 2–4% monthly churn; SaaS month-12 retention ~71%. Job tools are structurally 3–5× worse. Anyone quoting you generic SaaS benchmarks for this category is misleading you.

Why this kills the "make it a big company" fantasy

At 15% churn you replace your entire customer base every ~7 months. Growth requires acquisition to outrun a leaking bucket permanently. This is exactly why Teal needed $20.7M and still sits near $5M — they're buying customers faster than they lose them, which is expensive forever. It's also why Rezi's $149 lifetime deal is smarter than it looks: it converts a churning subscription into cash upfront, trading LTV ceiling for cash certainty.
